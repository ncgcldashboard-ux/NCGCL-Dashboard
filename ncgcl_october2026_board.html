Executive Snapshot

Financial & Investment data as at October 31, 2026 · Guarantee exposure per the October management accounts · Deal Pipeline, Products and Collaborations refreshed from the Guarantee Origination & Tracker · People data from the HR Metrics Model and the October People Pulse

Guarantee Book Grows to PKR 880.7 Mn — One New Issuance, No New MAGFA Signed
Outstanding guarantee exposure rose by PKR 50.0 Mn to PKR 880.7 Mn with the issuance of Damen via HBL FI on 14 October. Sanctioned limits are unchanged at PKR 11,737.5 Mn across nine counterparties, and utilisation of the sanctioned register edges up to 7.2% (from 6.8%). On conversion, Bank Alfalah is targeting mid-November after its Central Credit Committee, HBL MFB received the clean and marked-up MAGFA on 26–27 October and Bank of Punjab is awaiting its housing-facility MAGFA from its counsel. See Sections B and C.
Financial Performance — PKR 362.1 Mn YTD Net Profit, 17% Above Budget · Now Ahead of Prorated FY2025
YTD net profit of PKR 362.1 Mn is PKR 53.3 Mn (17%) above the internal budget and, for the first time this year, ahead of prorated FY2025 (PKR 360.3 Mn); the gap that stood at −5% in July closed in October. October on its own contributed PKR 41.6 Mn of net profit on PKR 78.6 Mn of other income, with finance cost at PKR 0.8 Mn (lease interest only) for the second month running. Guarantee revenue is PKR 2.16 Mn YTD against a PKR 25.5 Mn budget (8.5%), so earnings remain almost entirely investment income (Section F-11).
Investments — 11.70% MTD Yield, +65 bps Alpha, Ranked 5th Among Peer Funds · SBP Raises Policy Rate to 12.00%
The portfolio returned 11.70% MTD against an 11.05% benchmark (+65 bps) and 10.72% YTD. The SBP raised the policy rate by 50 bps to 12.00% on 26 October with Brent at US$117.64 (+15% in the month); the repricing was concentrated at the short end (1M–1Y PKRV +38 to +57 bps). Matured Alfalah Cash Fund-II proceeds were redeployed into PKR 300 Mn of Air Link Sukuk at 13.61%, lifting the Sukuk share to 18%. See Section G.
People — Headcount 18 of 33 After Two Voluntary Exits · October Pulse NPS 26.7 (June: 50.0)
Two voluntary separations in October, including the Company Secretary, took closing headcount to 18 of 33 and year-to-date voluntary attrition to 24.4% against a 12% benchmark. The October People Pulse (15 responses) scored an average of 4.16 / 5 (June 4.48) with NPS at 26.7. See Section H.
Engagement — ADB NBFI Liquidity Window, GCF B.46, IFAD REMIT Pakistan, World Bank Group / IFC Framework
October's partner work widened the channel: a US$285 Mn NBFI liquidity window proposal went to ADB (23 October, follow-up call 26 October); Karandaaz took three concept notes (NCGCL copied) to a working session with the GCF Secretariat during B.46 in Seoul (27 October); the final IFAD REMIT Pakistan submission was lodged on 20 October and preselected; and Karandaaz opened a World Bank Group / IFC collaboration framework. See Section E.

01 · ADB: NBFI Liquidity Window and Clean Air (CAFER) Plan Proposal Stage
Multilateral · CEO's Office with Research · 23–30 Oct 2026

NBFI window. Graded 80+ NBFIs from public financial statements in the internal risk model (initial calibration) and sized a facility on the SIDBI (India) and FIRA (Mexico) lines. Problem: NBFCs fund 2–5 year MSME, housing and leasing loans with mostly one-year bank lines; of PKR 335 Bn borrowed by the 82 lenders with audited accounts, 59% falls due within a year, they have no lender of last resort, and mutual funds hold PKR 4.5 Tn but almost no NBFI paper. Proposal: up to US$285 Mn in two tranches; tranche one US$146 Mn (about PKR 53 Bn) as Window A term loans PKR 25 Bn, Window B guaranteed bonds PKR 20 Bn and Window C standby PKR 8 Bn, plus a PKR 3 Bn digital-lender pool (Window D) and a PKR 5 Bn NCGCL-guaranteed bank-line pilot (B3). Eighteen lenders qualify today once data gaps close. The second tranche (about US$139 Mn) releases only on first-tranche performance gates. A possible second funding source, SIDBI-style placements by banks that miss priority-lending targets, would need SBP and IMF agreement.

Clean Air plan v0.2 (30 Oct). Eight windows across three phases: Phase 1 transport (e-2/3W US$4.5 Mn, EV supply chain US$2.0 Mn, e-bus US$4.0 Mn); Phase 2 industry, electric heat and rice machinery (US$8.0 / 1.0 / 2.5 Mn); Phase 3 cooking and solar (US$2.5 / 0.5 Mn). Modelled lending is US$81 Mn by month 18, US$329 Mn by month 45 and US$513 Mn by month 72; PM2.5 reduction at month 72 is 3,600 t adjusted (2,255 t excluding agriculture). Capital need is US$28.5 Mn at month 72; the TA ask has been reconciled to US$1.0 Mn.

Key insights: (1) all NBFI sizing is illustrative and rests on audited numbers not distorted by the advance-deposit-ratio tax; (2) Phase 1 tonnage is small (62 t/yr), and rice machinery alone is 48% of modelled tonnes on planning assumptions, so the agriculture window carries the environmental case; (3) open items before sending: whether US$25 Mn is capital or exposure, overlap of three windows with the GCF programmes, and coordination with the existing ADB project.
02 · GCF Programme Pack and the Karandaaz Counter-Guarantee Concept Stage
Climate Finance · CEO's Office · 15–29 Oct 2026 (for B.46, Seoul)

PIDCEP (industrial decarbonisation) started as a US$30 Mn ask (about 6.4 Mt lifetime abatement; roughly 7,000 boilers and 7.7 MtCO2 a year of process heat). On 28–29 October it was merged with e-mobility into one integrated programme (ask US$80 Mn, concept note v0.11) after a readiness review against a precedent project; GCF monitoring-and-evaluation guidance (business-as-usual baseline, grid emission factor, 2–5% M&E budget) and gender guidance (design-stage gender assessment and action plan) were applied. DSNF (distributed solar-to-night storage) stands at a US$25 Mn ask with 1.63 Mt modelled abatement. PIWSCP (industrial water circularity) has a funding proposal V.1.0 with annexes and a model.

Karandaaz counter-guarantee structure (21 Oct). Six Karandaaz concepts were reviewed and seven structures scored. The preferred option is a GCF-funded PKR green counter-guarantee trust managed by Karandaaz (US$40 Mn) with US$8 Mn of fee support and US$5 Mn of TA (US$53 Mn in total); a layered alternative puts US$25 Mn of first loss and a US$50 Mn GCF guarantee behind roughly US$800 Mn of lending. A credit-model re-run found window default rates of 6.6–7.4% and losses of 11–17% of originations, so the required NCGCL fee is well above the 1.0–1.5% first assumed; the recommended trust share was raised to 75% with fees at 2.0–2.5%, leaving a gap of about US$7.7 Mn over phase one for the Board or GCF to cover.

Key insights: (1) the programme designs are concept-stage and modelled; the loss assumptions in the bond model sit below NCGCL's own calibration and are flagged in the notes; (2) integrating industry and e-mobility lifts the ask but removes duplicated Karandaaz and ADB asks; (3) the open question for B.46 was the scope of Karandaaz's accreditation, which determines whether the counter-guarantee can be hosted there.

05 · SME Risk Cover Economics: the SBP First-Loss Pricing Note Policy Note
Policy · PM Office note · 27 Oct 2026

Analyses the SBP risk-cover scheme's free SME first-loss cover. At the policy target of PKR 2 Tn of SME lending, free first-loss cover would grow from PKR 62 Bn to about PKR 210 Bn; a fair fee is estimated at about 3.9% and the implied subsidy rises from roughly PKR 16 Bn to PKR 55 Bn a year. The note proposes a redesign toward priced, tiered cover.

Key insight: this is the economics behind Meezan's October answer that it does not need second-loss cover while the free first loss is in place. Pricing a second loss alongside a free first loss is only viable where the first loss is exhausted, in segments the scheme does not reach, or in NBFC channels it excludes.
